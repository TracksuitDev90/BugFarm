# Critter Farm

A cosy picture-book terrarium. One big glass tank with a sandy desert, a pond
and a grassy jungle, full of small critters going about their lives. Every
critter has a job, like everyone in Busytown: bees make honey, beetles and worms
make compost, butterflies leave silk behind. Every grown-up has a dream of its
own, too. Collect what they make and send it to the neighbours who write in
asking for it; they pay in coins, become friends, and sell the farm the
wonderful things they make. What the farm makes depends on how it's kept:
honey tastes of the flowers the bees visited, lilies bloom the colour of the
water, and masters of their trade make prize goods. Now and then a neighbour
would love to give one of the critters a home, visitors drop in from the
garden outside with presents and ideas for the tank, and the critters can
dress up in hats from every trade in Busytown. Look after the critters,
rearrange their world, and run gentle experiments to see what happens.

- **Runs in any modern browser** on phones, tablets and desktops. Nothing to
  install. It can also be added to the home screen and played offline.
- **Small and quick to load.** About 260 KB of gzipped JavaScript and two
  small fonts. All the art is drawn in code, so there are no image downloads.
- **Smooth on old hardware.** The static scenery is cached, critters are
  pre-rendered sprites, and the simulation runs at a fixed 30 steps a second.
  If a device struggles, the quality setting drops on its own.
- **Saves itself.** The tank is saved to the browser every 20 seconds and
  whenever you switch away. When you come back, it catches up on the time
  you were gone (up to about a day of tank time, and gently).

The art takes after Richard Scarry: ink outlines, washes of colour,
rosy cheeks, little hats, houses made from teapots, boots and apples, and a
town full of animals dressed for their jobs.
Everything inside the glass is painted like a picture-book page: watercolour
washes that bloom and pool at their edges, ink stippling in the sand, pencil
hatching in the tunnels, a bold ink line, all on cold-press paper. Sunlight
falls through the glass in soft shafts with dust drifting in them, dappled
light comes down through the jungle canopy, and rays sway under the pond.
The pond has depth: looking in through the front glass, the water darkens
toward the bottom and goes back and back, with fronds and stones fading into
a blue-green haze behind the fish (they drift past more slowly than the near
things as you look around), the gravel floor receding into it, and the
silvery underside of the surface just below the waterline. Fish swim nearer
and further too, smaller and hazier when they're far back, and come up to
the front to eat.
Every so often a soft breeze rolls through the tank: the grass bows, the
flowers and palms lean, the vines swing, leaves and dandelion fluff blow
about, sand lifts off the dunes and the pond ruffles up. Under the water the
filter keeps a slow current turning, so the eelgrass waves, specks drift
past and fallen leaves float round the pond.
Nobody is scary, and nothing dies horribly. When a critter's time is up it
floats away under a little halo and a forget-me-not grows where it rested.

## The critters

Each species has its own needs, diet, comfort range and habits. They decide
what to do from moment to moment based on what they can see, smell, feel and
remember.

| Zone | Who lives there | What to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desert | Ants (garden, harvester and honeypot colonies), crickets, a gecko, beetles | The ants run a real colony. The queen lays eggs, nurses raise the brood, and diggers carve tunnels and flat-floored rooms and pile the sand into a mound. The queen walks slowly among her eggs, and moves to whichever room is the right warmth for them, with workers escorting her and carrying the brood after her. Scouts patrol the tunnels and rush to anything new (dig a hollow by the nest and they'll make it a room), a couple of guards stand at the door and shoo away big visitors, and well-fed ants feed hungry sisters mouth to mouth. Every colony digs in its own style (steep shafts or long galleries, winding or straight), so no two nests look alike. Colonies come in three kinds: garden ants herd aphids, big-headed harvester ants stock a granary and pile spare seeds by the door, and honeypot ants swell up golden with nectar and fill jars of desert honey. Each kind you adopt digs its own nest. Ants walk on floors, walls and ceilings, and stop to touch antennae when they meet. Foragers lay scent trails to food and recruit their nestmates. Crickets chirp faster when it is warm. The gecko basks under the lamp and stalks crickets. |
| Pond | Goldfish (common, comet, fantail, black moor, oranda, shubunkin...), guppies, neon tetras, koi, pea puffers, an axolotl, frogs (from eggs to tadpoles to froglets), a turtle, pond snails, shrimp, dragonflies (nymphs underwater, then up and away) | The fish swim in loose shoals at every depth, flicking their tails and coasting, darting off now and then, playing tag, and dozing among the weeds or tucked up in the castle. Their whole bodies stay in the water, fins and tails included: they glide just clear of the gravel, slide along a sloping bank rather than nosing into it, tip their noses down to nibble what's on the bottom and then swim off again, and spread out through the water rather than bunching up in the same few spots. They learn when and where you feed them: open the Feed tool and they crowd up to the top, and at the usual time of day the hungry ones wait at the usual spot. The turtle basks under the pond's lamp, floating at the top with its shell in the warm air, then goes for long swims and comes up for air. Pond snails glide all over the glass and hang under the water's skin, and shrimp swim up in little hops and climb the water plants. Warm water holds less oxygen, so overfeeding or a heat wave sends fish gasping at the surface. Tadpoles turn into frogs. Guppies skim the surface for flies, neon tetras school shoulder to shoulder and glow after dark, koi rummage in the gravel (and sometimes find a lost coin), pea puffers puff up into prickly balls when startled and keep the snails in check, and the axolotl strolls along the bottom smiling. Dragonfly nymphs hunt along the bottom, then climb out and zoom off on four wings. |
| Jungle | Ladybugs, aphids, butterflies (monarch, blue morpho, tiger swallowtail, pink rose, cabbage white, and rare peacocks, glasswings and emerald swallowtails), moths (luna, rosy maple, polyphemus), snails, bumblebees, earthworms, fireflies, grasshoppers, pill bugs | A misty rainforest of palms, monsteras and bromeliads. The vines hanging from the lid are living plants: pothos in a clay pot, string of pearls on a moss ball, a woody liana looped between two hooks, Spanish moss on a twig, hoya, passion flower, morning glory, pitcher plants and string of hearts. Each one roots as a little cutting, grows down over a day or two, lives a week or two, then yellows, drops its leaves and withers away. Most new vines are cuttings of a neighbour, so the canopy has runs of the same plant with the odd newcomer. A dry jungle browns them; they swing from their hooks like rope in the breeze. Ants farm aphids for honeydew and ladybugs hunt them. Bees fly pollen home to their hotel to make honey. Worms surface when it rains. At night the fireflies' blinking slowly falls into sync, and moths loop round every lamp. |

Fruit flies are uninvited guests who show up when fruit is left to rot.

Many critters come in **varieties**, and the Field guide keeps a collection
of every one you've met (rare ones included), so there's always another to
find.

### Flying free

Butterflies, moths, dragonflies, bees and the rest fly like flyers. Each kind
has its own cruising height: butterflies and dragonflies up near the lid,
bees at flower height, fireflies low. On a long trip they climb up to cruise
and come down near the end, so flights arc over the tank instead of skimming
it; they look ahead and keep clear of hills and banks; they take off steeply
and land on high perches. Butterflies flap, then glide on open wings, and
ride the warm air rising under a heat lamp in lazy spirals. Two butterflies
that meet may dance up into the sky together. Dragonflies dart, stop dead to
hover, and dart again. Moths at night can't keep away from a lamp. Hardly
any trip is a straight line: a butterfly meanders and loops the loop, a bee
swings round in long arcs, a dragonfly zigzags.

## What the farm is for

Every critter has a job, shown on its card along with how much it has made,
and every job does something real in the tank. When a critter isn't eating,
sleeping or hiding, it works:

| Good | Who makes it | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Honey** | Bumblebees, honeypot ants | Bees fill their pollen baskets at the flowers and fly them home to the bee hotel. Its nesting holes fill with honey, and a jar appears beside it. The same pollen raises new bees. Honeypot ants store nectar in their golden tummies and leave a jar of desert honey by the nest door. |
| **Compost** | Beetles, earthworms | Big leafy plants drop old leaves. Beetles pat leaves and leftovers into a ball, roll it over to a plant and bury it. Worms turn leaves into castings at the surface. Either way the soil gets richer and plants grow faster. |
| **Seeds** | Bees, butterflies, moths, fireflies, harvester ants | Pollinated flowers ripen into seed heads (moths and fireflies pollinate by night). Harvester ants pile their spare seeds by the nest door. |
| **Silk** | Butterflies, moths | A butterfly leaves its empty chrysalis behind when it hatches, and a moth its silk cocoon. |
| **Water lilies** | Goldfish, tetras, koi, guppies, pond snails, shrimp | The fish feed the lilies and the snails and shrimp keep the water clear, so the lilies bloom. |

Goods sparkle in the tank until you tap them to collect them. Anything you
leave still does some good: seeds sow themselves and compost melts into the
soil.

### Flavours and prize goods

Every good takes something of where it came from, so what you plant and how
you keep the tank shows in what the farm makes:

| Good | What decides its flavour |
| --- | --- |
| **Honey** | The flowers the bees visited. If half the pollen in the comb came from one kind of flower, the jar is that flower's honey (clover, lavender, orchid, moonflower and the rest); a bit of everything makes wildflower honey. Plant a patch of one flower near the bee hotel to choose. The honeypot ants make desert honey. The bee hotel's card says what the next jar will taste of. |
| **Seeds** | The flower that ripened them: sunflower, poppy, cornflower... (harvester ants pile wildflower seeds). |
| **Water lilies** | The water. Lilies bloom white in cool water (under 20°C), pink in mild water and golden in warm water (25°C and up), so the pond heater decides. Very clear water with a school of neon tetras now and then gives a rare blue star lily, and a lotus gives lotus blooms. |
| **Silk** | Whoever spun it: monarch, blue morpho, swallowtail, rose, snow-white, luna, rosy maple and the rest, with the rare peacock, glasswing and emerald silks from the butterfly house. |
| **Compost** | A beetle's compost ball, or a heap of worm castings. |

**Prize goods** wear a little gold rosette. A jar of one flower's honey made
mostly by master bees (whose dreams came true) is a prize jar; master
recyclers often make prize compost; healthy, comfy plants in rich, well
composted soil grow prize seed heads now and then; a crystal-clear pond with
well tended lilies grows prize blooms; and a caterpillar that grew up well
fed and healthy leaves prize silk. The basket on the Orders tab lists every
flavour you have (tap one to hear where it comes from).

Seven newer flowers give the bees more to choose from: desert poppies and
lavender in the desert, cornflowers, bluebells and hibiscus in the jungle,
yellow irises by the pond (they unlock as the zones grow), and orchids from
Flora Petal at the Market. The other critters' jobs matter too: ladybugs guard the plants from
aphids; frogs, guppies, geckos and dragonflies keep the fruit flies down; pea
puffers keep the snails from overrunning the pond; the crickets' night chorus
lulls the whole tank to sleep, so everyone wakes up rested; grasshoppers mow
the grass and drop the clippings for the recyclers; the ants' tunnels let air
into the sand so desert plants grow better; koi turn up lost coins; and the
axolotl is so loved in Busytown that every order pays extra.

### Dreams

Every grown-up critter also has a dream of its own that grows out of its job:
a bee wants to visit thirty flowers, a koi to find three lucky coins, a
cricket to star in the night chorus, a butterfly to dance in the sky. Its card
shows the dream and how close it is. When it comes true the critter wears a
gold star, becomes a master of its trade (it works a quarter faster from then
on) and Busytown sends a couple of coins to celebrate.

## Busytown and the Market

Thirteen neighbours in Busytown write to the farm for its goods: Baker Bramble
wants honey, Tilly Thimble the tailor wants silk, Gus Greenthumb the gardener
wants compost, Flora Petal wants lilies, Miss Penny's class wants seeds, and
Pip the postman, Sprocket the inventor, Odette at the pet shop, Bertie Bricks
the builder and the rest all have their reasons. Each of them is drawn in the
book's style: a bear in a baker's cap, a toad mayor in a top hat, an owl
teacher in spectacles, a bear builder in a hard hat.

![A letter from Tilly Thimble the tailor, a grey tabby cat in spectacles and a purple dress, asking for a silk chrysalis for the mayor's parade scarf. It pays 12 coins, 3 stars and treats, and says that after two more orders she'll sell the farm a butterfly house](docs/letter.png)

A new farm is asked who lives there: your own name or your town's. The
letters are addressed to it ("Dear Juniper" or "Dear friends at Maple
Hollow"), the thank-yous use it, and it's on the brass plate on the front of
the tank. It can be changed in Settings.

When a letter comes it pops up with a portrait of whoever wrote it. Their
letters wait on the clipboard's **Orders** tab, and the mailbox flag goes up.
Send an order and they reply, delighted, with **coins**, thank-you stars, a
basket of treats for the critters, and a **heart** of friendship. Coins are
spent at the Market, stars bring Busytown's gifts (below), and the treats are
the critters' favourite snacks, dropped into the zone whose critters made
the goods. Tap any of them on a letter to see what it's for. A letter you
can't fill yet says **Check back soon**: tap it to see what's still missing,
where it comes from and how long they'll wait. **Not now** passes on a letter
(they'll write again another time, and a new letter comes along sooner).

Once the farm is up and running the letters get more particular. Every
neighbour has favourite flavours: Baker Bramble's grandmother's recipe
needs clover honey, Doctor Dot swears by lavender honey for a tickly cough,
the bride wants snow-white silk, the mayor wants golden lilies for the town
hall fountain. A letter for a particular flavour pays half as much again (and
waits a day longer). Once you've made a prize good, someone entering the
Busytown show may ask for your very best, and pays double. Prize goods on an
everyday order earn a little extra, and the farm stand never sells them.

Now and then a neighbour would love to give one of your critters a home: a
cricket to sing at story time in Miss Penny's class, ladybugs for Farmer
Furrow's beans, a comet goldfish for Odette's shop window. They only ask for
critters you have a few of, more often when the tank is crowded, and you
choose who goes (from the letter, or from the critter's own card, which
gets a "Send to..." button). Off they float under a balloon, and the
neighbour writes to say how they've settled in.

Every neighbour makes something special and sells it, at the **Busytown
Market** (the coin purse, top right), to farms that have helped them enough.
None of them are just for show:

![The Busytown Market: striped stalls for Baker Bramble, Gus Greenthumb, Flora Petal, Mayor Mossbottom, Farmer Furrow and Pip Parcel, each with their portrait, hearts of friendship and their ware (busy buns, garden sprinklers, moonflower seeds, a town notice board, a rain barrel, a mail dragonfly) with its price in coins](docs/market.jpg)

### Dressing up

Tap a critter and press **Dress up** to choose something for it to wear from
the wardrobe, and its colour. The picture shows how it looks before you
decide. Crickets, grasshoppers, geckos, beetles, goldfish, koi, frogs,
turtles, ladybugs, butterflies, snails, bees, earthworms, fireflies, pill
bugs and the axolotl can all dress up. Hats come from the Market (above),
and two things only come from visitors: Kip the kingfisher's feather cap and
Hector the hermit crab's pearl necklace. Anything the wardrobe doesn't have
yet is shown as a silhouette that says where to find it.

## Visitors from the garden

When the tank is happy and lively, someone from the garden outside drops in
now and then. They come in over the side of the glass (or flutter down
through the lid), potter about the zone they like for a few hours and go
home. Who comes depends on how you've built their world, and every visitor
brings something:

| Visitor | Comes when | Brings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hazel the hedgehog | the jungle floor has pill bugs or beetles, fallen leaves and a home or two (at night) | rakes the leaves into a **leaf pile**: a home for 4 small critters that drops a leaf for the recyclers every morning |
| Mortimer the mole | the jungle soil is rich and there are earthworms | digs **tunnels** under the jungle, with a molehill at each way in; plants in airy soil grow faster |
| Rosie the robin | the jungle is full of flowers of many kinds (by day) | sows a patch of **wildflowers**, often ones you can't plant yet, and from then on you can |
| Kip the kingfisher | the pond is crystal clear and full of fish, with reeds to perch on (by day) | a **feather cap** for the wardrobe |
| Wilma the water vole | there are water lilies in a clean pond, and reeds along the bank | plants a **lotus**, whose blooms you pick like water lilies (only Wilma has lotuses) |
| Hector the hermit crab | the desert by the pond is warm and there's a seashell or treasure chest in the water | a **pearl necklace** for the wardrobe |
| Tobias the tortoise | the desert is full of plants, with a rock or two (by day) | rolls stones into a cool, shady **stone den** for 4 desert critters |

A visitor who wants to change the tank always asks first. Their card pops
up with their idea, an "!" floats over them until you answer (tap them to
hear it again), and if they go home before you've decided they leave it as a
note in the **Visitors' Book** (the Journal's Visitors tab). Every change is
for the better, and saying no is fine: they'll ask again another time. Once
a visitor has built a leaf pile or a stone den you can place more of them
from Decor, and anything they've sown you can plant from Plants. On later
visits they bring their present again until they've done all they can, and
after that a few lucky coins they found on the way.

The Visitors' Book shows the **tank cheer** (how comfy, fed and healthy
everyone is, how the plants are doing, how clear the pond is and how many
kinds of critter live there), everyone who's been, and a hint for how to
tempt the ones who haven't. A happier tank gets visitors more often. If
you've been away for a few hours, someone may have come by while you were
gone and left a present or a note.

The decorations all have a purpose too. The houses, logs and hidey-holes are
homes; the bee hotel makes honey; bubblers add air; the park bench makes for
extra-restful naps; crickets sing from the fence so the night chorus carries;
the windmill pumps a trickle of water into its corner; the farm sign brings
letters a little sooner and the mailbox holds one more; the banana car lures
fruit flies away from your snacks; and bookworms who live in the little
library work a fifth faster.

## Growing your farm

A new farm starts small: each zone has just two kinds of critter (ants and
crickets in the desert, goldfish and pond snails in the pond, snails and
butterflies in the jungle). Each zone has a level and three or four keeper goals.
Most mix looking after the zone ("keep the desert comfy for 6 hours", "rinse
out the pond filter") with something its critters make or send ("beetles roll
2 balls of compost", "pick a water lily in bloom", "send an order to
Busytown"). Finishing them levels the zone up
and unlocks new critters, snacks, plants and decorations: beetles, harvester
ants, a gecko and honeypot ants in the desert; shrimp, guppies, frogs, neon
tetras, a turtle and dragonflies in the pond; and worms, pill bugs, aphids,
ladybugs, moths, bees, fireflies and grasshoppers in the jungle. Koi, pea
puffers and the axolotl come from the pet shop at the Market. The
**Sandbox** choices on the welcome screen (or in Settings) unlock everything
at once.

The tank doesn't stay put, either. The weather outside changes every couple
of days (warm and cool spells, heat waves, cold snaps, dry and muggy days),
which shifts the room temperature. Misters drink from a water bottle that
runs dry, the pond slowly evaporates below its fill line, the filter sponge
clogs with gunk, and leafy jungle plants make the air more humid as they
grow. And when a zone fills up, Bertie the builder can make it bigger.

A new farm starts calm and the pace picks up gently. Letters come a while
apart (the first few ask for things the farm can make soon, and a letter
nobody can fill is withdrawn after a couple of days), the weather saves its
heat waves and cold snaps until the farm is a few days old, and discoveries,
news and tips take turns, one at a time with a breather between them.
Whatever there wasn't room for is waiting in the journal, and its button
gets a dot.

None of that comes as a pop-up. Instead:

- **The keeper's clipboard** (top right) lists what needs doing, most urgent
  first, each with a one-tap fix: refill, rinse, top up, clean, collect, or a
  jump to the right climate control or snack. Its badge shows how many things
  want attention (and doesn't blink at something that comes and goes in a
  moment). It also says when a zone is getting crowded. The Orders tab holds Busytown's letters and your basket of
  goods. Each zone's tab shows its goals, progress and what's next.
- **The equipment explains itself.** Tap a heat lamp, mister, the pond heater
  or filter, or a thermometer to see what it is, what it does, how it's doing
  and its controls. Zoom in and each one has a little name tag.
- **Comfy ranges.** Every zone works out the temperature (and dampness) that
  suits whoever actually lives there, and says who's too hot or too dry.
  The clock colours each zone's temperature, the Climate panel shows the
  range on a gauge, and strip thermometers on the glass show it in the tank.
- **The forecast.** The clipboard and the Climate panel say what weather is
  coming, so a heat wave is never a surprise.

| **Coin purse** | Next to the clipboard. Your Busytown coins, and the way into the Market. |

### Homes

The cottages, the boot, the teapot, the library, the hollow log and the
tipped flowerpot are real homes. Critters move in (each home has a few
rooms), go home to sleep, hide inside from the midday heat or a cold snap,
dash home when something startles them, and pop their heads out of the
door to see if it's safe. Chimneys smoke and the teapot's spout steams on a
chilly evening, and at night a lamp glows in the window when someone's in.
Sunk in the pond, the castle, the seashell, the treasure chest, the diver's
helmet and the clay pipe are hidey-holes for the fish, tadpoles, shrimp and
pond snails. Tap a home to see who lives there.

Everything you put down settles into the tank as if it had always been
there: it beds a little way into the sand, gravel or soil, leans a touch with
the slope, and the ground heaps up round its foot in a drift of whatever
it's standing in (pebbles in the pond, sand in the desert), with pond weed,
moss or grass taking hold there. Put one down half up a rock face and it
shuffles along to where it can sit level.

The lens button in the top right switches between overlays for temperature,
dampness, ant scent trails and what each critter is thinking. The speed control
offers pause, 1×, 3× and 8×. On a keyboard, `Space` pauses, `1` to `8` pick
the tools and `Esc` closes panels.

### Experiments to try

- **Overfeed the pond.** Leftovers spoil, the water clouds over, oxygen drops,
  and the fish come up to gulp air. Use *Clean pond* to recover.
- **Underfeed everyone.** Watch tummies rumble and critters search further
  afield. Hungry hunters start eyeing their neighbours.
- **Heat wave in the desert.** The ants retreat deep into their tunnels. Plants
  wilt and some critters seal up and wait for better days.
- **Cold snap.** Crickets chirp more slowly, the reptiles get sluggish, and the
  ladybugs huddle together.
- **Warm or chill the pond.** Fish are happiest in the middle of the range.
  Warm water holds less air.
- **Rainy day.** The earthworms come up to the surface.
- **Leave fruit to rot.** Fruit flies appear.
- **Dig a drain.** Dig a tunnel from the bottom of the pond into the sand.
  The water pours down it and fills the tunnel from the bottom up, and the
  pond level drops. A small puddle cut off from the pond soaks away.
- **Change their world.** Dig a hole, pile up a hill or flood a new channel off
  the pond with the Shape tool. Critters come to see what's new, fish swim off
  to explore the new water (round corners and all), and ant scouts check out
  anything dug near their nest.
- **Feed the fish at the same time every day.** After a few days they wait for
  you at the usual spot.
- **Drop more food than anyone can eat.** Watch who shares it, who carries a
  bit home, and who leaves it be.
- **Tap the glass by a house.** Critters with a home nearby dash inside, then
  peek out of the door when it seems safe again.
- **Let the ants dig a few rooms, then change the desert heat.** The queen
  moves house to a comfier room, with her workers fussing round her.
- **Add a predator.** The gecko eats crickets, frogs catch flies, and ladybugs
  eat aphids. The world stays mostly peaceful, but the food chain is real.

## Run it locally

You need Node 18 or newer.

```sh
npm install          # esbuild for the build, Playwright for the browser tests
npm run dev          # serve the source at http://localhost:8080
npm run build        # bundle into dist/ (one hashed JS file, offline service worker)
npm test             # headless browser smoke test of the source (npm test -- dist for the build)
```

To save bandwidth, `PLAYWRIGHT_SKIP_BROWSER_DOWNLOAD=1 npm install` skips the
browser download when you only want to build.

Other tools in `tools/`:

| --- | --- |
| `node tools/simtest.mjs [days] [seed]` | Runs the simulation headless and prints a population log and timing. |
| `node tools/fuzz.mjs [days] [seed]` | Has a random player feed, sculpt, adopt and change the climate, and reports any broken state. |

## How it works

```
src/
  config.js            world size (and how much bigger it's been built), grid, caps
  world/               terrain grid (sand/soil/rock/gravel/clay), flowing water,
                       environment (zone climate, pond chemistry, day/night),
                       the breeze and the pond's current, the jungle's living
                       vines, how far the water is from the ground (for
                       swimmers' bodies), spatial hash, A* pathfinding, the
                       World itself
  entities/            Creature (needs, life stages, movement), Plant, Food, Decor,
                       Good, Visitor (a visitor's comings and goings)
  ai/                  utility brain, shared behaviours, food/prey/mate search,
                       ant colony (castes, brood, digging, scent trails, aphid herding)
  data/                species, foods, plants, decor, names, discoveries,
                       progression (what each zone level unlocks), goods and
                       jobs and flavours, Busytown's neighbours, the Market's
                       wares, critters' dreams, visitors, outfits
  art/                 procedural ink-and-watercolour drawing of everything
                       (critters, plants, vines, equipment, goods, the
                       townsfolk's portraits, visitors, hats, the Market's
                       gadgets...),
                       paper grain and watercolour washes
  render/              sprite cache, chunked terrain and water, the pond's
                       depth, where decorations meet the ground, light
                       shafts, particles, drifting specks and wind-blown
                       leaves, frame
  ui/                  camera, touch input, toolbar, sheets, cards, journal,
                       Busytown's letters and the Market, the herald (paces
                       what pops up)
  sim/                 away catch-up, journal, care (comfy ranges and the
                       to-do list), progress (zone levels and goals), orders
                       (Busytown's letters, coins and friendship), market
                       (buying wares), dreams, visitors (who comes, their
                       offers and presents), equipment (what each gadget is),
                       the keeper's name, tank (building it bigger)
  save/                localStorage save, backup, export/import
  audio/               tiny procedural sound effects (WebAudio, no files)
```

- **Brains.** Every critter senses its surroundings, scores the behaviours its
  species knows (forage, hunt, flee, bask, hide, court, chirp, pollinate,
  bring pollen home, recycle, estivate, go home, say hello to a friend, share
  or keep spare food and so on) against its current needs, and runs the best
  one. An errand under way holds on unless something more pressing comes up. Work scores highest
  when a critter is fed and comfy, so a well-kept farm is a busy one. Every
  critter has its own pace, and now and then a passing whim (a stroll, a nose
  about, a natter, a sit at home) nudges it out of its usual routine.
  Ants have their own colony-level task allocation on top of that. Species and
  life stages are mostly data in `src/data/species.js`.
- **Water.** Every cell holds an amount of water. Water falls into room
  below it and spreads sideways, and within one connected body it moves
  from the top to the lowest opening, which is enough for draining, filling
  tunnels from the bottom and levelling out both sides of a U-bend. The sim
  goes to sleep once the water is still, so a calm pond costs nothing.
- **Movement.** Ants follow smoothed paths and feel which way the ground
  is, so they stand on floors, cling to walls and walk along ceilings.
  Flyers steer toward a target with a heading of their own that wanders,
  climb to their species' cruising height on long trips, look ahead to keep
  clear of the ground, and are lifted by warm air under the lamps (see
  `FLIGHT` in `src/entities/creature.js`).
  Walkers ease in and out of moving, stop to sniff and look round, change
  their minds and dawdle or hurry, every critter swivels to turn round, and
  hops stretch and land with a squash. Every trip a critter sets off on gets
  a shape of its own, picked by what kind of critter it is (see
  `src/entities/course.js`): swimmers and flyers weave, sweep round in arcs,
  meander, zigzag or loop the loop, and pond snails leave wavy trails on the
  glass; on foot, a trip has stops for a look round, a pace that comes and
  goes and now and then a step back to sniff at something just passed. Ants
  pause to wave their feelers, sway from side to side down roomy tunnels and
  search for food in short casts that double back. Only urgent trips go
  straight: fleeing, hunting, a starving dash for food, a gasp for air, or a
  short hop. Fish swim in bursts and coast in between, and each one has
  a favourite depth that drifts over the day, and they pick places to go
  that nobody else is already at. A swimmer's body is a few circles fitted
  to its drawing (every look, tail beat and pose, by `tools/hulls.mjs`), and
  all of it has to stay in the water. `src/world/clearance.js` keeps a
  signed distance field of how far every spot round the water is from the
  ground (worked out again only when the ground there changes), so each
  step the body is turned and flipped as it's drawn, pushed straight out of
  any ground it touches, and stops swimming into it, sliding along a bank
  instead. Places to swim to are moved to where the whole body fits with a
  little open water round it, and swimmers keep a body's width apart. A fish
  buried by the keeper's sculpting wriggles out into the water nearby. An
  amphibian in water too small to swim in wades along the bottom instead.
- **Noticing changes.** The water is mapped from the cells that really hold
  water (redrawn whenever it moves), so swimmers can go anywhere there's
  water, including channels and flooded tunnels, and they find a route through
  the water when the way isn't straight. Digging, filling and new water are
  noted as changes worth exploring. Ants remember where they've been lately.
- **Breeze and current.** Gusts are fronts that travel across the tank and
  die away behind them. Plants bend with a shear (the base stays put, the
  tip leans), and the critters clinging to them use the same bend, so they
  stay on their leaves. The vines are pendulums pushed by the smoothed
  breeze in real time (so they're just as smooth at 8×), and bend along their
  length (each is drawn in sheared slices that meet exactly). The pond's
  current is one slow loop driven by the
  filter. It carries swimmers, bubbles, floating flakes and frogspawn a
  little, and stops short of the shallows so food never strands out of reach.
- **Decorations settle in.** `seatOf` in `src/world/world.js` rests a
  decoration's base on the ground under its whole footprint (not just its
  middle), sinks it in a little and leans it with the slope; placing one
  shuffles it along to level ground if it's put down on a rock face.
  `src/render/footing.js` then puts the painted ground back in front of its
  sunken base (copied from the cached background, so it matches exactly),
  heaps a drift of the same material against it, shades where it sits and
  grows a little weed or grass round its foot. The drift is painted once into
  a small canvas of its own and redrawn only when something changes.
- **Simulation and rendering are separate.** `src/world` and `src/entities`
  never touch the DOM, so the whole tank runs headless in Node for the tests and
  for the catch-up after you've been away.
- **Rendering on a budget.** The terrain and the water are drawn in
  32-cell chunks with smoothed marching squares and redrawn only where they
  change. The room, backdrop, light shafts, terrain and frame are cached
  into a single background image. Critters are drawn once per pose and size
  bucket into a sprite cache, painted finish included.
- **The pond's depth.** Behind everything in the water, clipped to the
  water's own shape, is a far wall of water darkening with depth, a lit band
  of gravel floor receding into it, and two bands of faint fronds and stones
  on seamless tiles that move with parallax. A swimmer's distance back from
  the glass is only for drawing: further back is smaller and fainter, and
  far ones are drawn first.
- **A bigger tank.** The tank's size is read from storage before anything
  else starts (`TANK` in `src/config.js`), since every grid and cache is
  sized to it. Building works on the save: `expandSave` in `src/sim/tank.js`
  lets new columns into the ground and water (new land at the desert's or
  jungle's far side, the pond's middle copied across), moves everything to
  the right of them along, and the game reloads into the bigger tank. Things
  the game places itself are given in the original tank's coordinates and
  put in place with `X()`.
- **Saving.** The save is compact JSON (about 50 KB, with run-length-encoded
  terrain, plus goods, the basket and Busytown's orders) in `localStorage`,
  with a backup copy, and the tank's size alongside it. Settings can export
  and import saves as files. (A tank saved back when the farm was called Bug
  Farm is found under its old name and moves across the next time it saves.)

### Adding things

The catalogs are plain data, and most additions are a single line:

- **Food:** add an entry to `src/data/foods.js`. Pick an existing `shape` for
  its art, and give it `tags` (fruit, leaf, seed, protein, nectar…) so the
  critters know whether to eat it.
- **Plant:** add an entry to `src/data/plants.js` with a `kind`, colours and
  comfort ranges. A flowering one gets a honey flavour and a seed flavour of
  its own automatically; give it a `honey` colour (and a `taste` if its name
  doesn't make a good adjective).
- **Decoration:** add an entry to `src/data/decor.js`, plus a draw function in
  `src/art/decor.js` if it needs new art (drawn standing on its base at
  (0, 0)). It settles into the ground by itself.
- **Critter:** add a species to `src/data/species.js`, built from existing
  behaviours, and an entry in `CREATURE_ART` in `src/art/creatures.js`.
  Flyers also get a `WINGS` entry there (hinge point, length, beat rate) and
  a `FLIGHT` style in `src/entities/creature.js`. Swimmers get a `body`:
  add them to the list in `tools/hulls.mjs`, run it and paste in the circles
  it prints (and again whenever their art changes). Give it a job in
  `src/data/goods.js` and a dream or two in `src/data/dreams.js`. Named
  varieties are palettes with a `name`.
- **Unlock:** list a critter, food, plant or decoration under a zone level in
  `src/data/progression.js`. Anything not listed is available from the start.
- **Job or good:** jobs and goods are in `src/data/goods.js`. A new good also
  needs art in `src/art/goods.js` and something in the world that makes it
  (see `addGood` in `src/world/world.js`).
- **Neighbour:** add someone to `src/data/neighbours.js` with the goods they
  want and why, what they say to thank you, and a `look` for their portrait
  (an animal, colours, clothes, a hat and a few extras; see
  `src/art/townsfolk.js`).
- **Ware:** add it to `src/data/market.js` with who sells it, the hearts it
  takes and its price, then give it its job (a perk the world checks with
  `world.owns(id)`, a decoration with a flag, a critter, a plant or
  something to wear).
- **Outfit:** add it to `src/data/outfits.js`, draw it in `drawHat` (or
  `drawNeckwear`) in `src/art/ink.js`, and sell it as an `outfit` ware.
- **Visitor:** add one to `src/data/visitors.js` with what tempts it (`want`),
  how it moves and what it brings, and draw it in `src/art/visitors.js`.
